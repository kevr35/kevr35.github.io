abstract: "Decomposing Hamiltonians into classically tractable fragments is central to quantum simulation. One such way of doing so is the contextual subspace decomposition, which expresses the Hamiltonian with respect to non-contextual sets of Paulis. The conditions for contextuality rely on the anticommutation and commutation of Pauli operators; it is therefore natural to make an explicit connection between non-contextuality and fermionic physics. Theoretical work is centered around two theorems: in the first, we consider non-contextual Pauli Hamiltonians and show that they are free-fermion-disguised. In the second we write these Pauli non-contextual Hamiltonians in terms of Majorana and Dirac fermionic operators, showing that they carry a Jordan--Wigner-like fermion-to-qubit encoding. These two central theorems are formalized in the proof assistant language Lean. We use this to describe the space of fermionic non-contextual Hamiltonians, and highlight their relation to the more general class of free-fermion-disguised Hamiltonians. We further develop a heuristic algorithm to find line-graph based free-fermion fragments and their connected components of molecular Hamiltonians. We benchmark our algorithms against non-contextual searches for encoded molecular Hamiltonians and find comparable performance in identifying free-fermion solvable components. Our work has implications for what fragments to consider as useful for quantum simulation."
